# 4C AI-native Equity Intelligence — Customer Pilot Plan

Project ID: LV-2026-001  
Version: 1.0  
Status: Plan; no pilot customer is yet confirmed.

## 1. Pilot objective

Test whether founding teams can use an AI-native product to explore and compare initial equity-structure scenarios with less friction, clearer assumptions and acceptable professional quality.

The pilot is not designed to prove the entire historical 4C model or the long-term governance product.

## 2. Pilot cohort

Target 1–2 real founding teams for the first controlled pilot. Keep the cohort narrow:
- pre-seed / early-stage founding team;
- 2–5 founders or core founding participants;
- no active shareholder dispute;
- no complicated cross-border or regulated securities structure;
- willing to provide structured, minimized information and participate in feedback.

Potential buyer groups to interview: founders, incubators / accelerators and startup service providers. Do not assume they are the same buyer.

## 3. Discovery before pilot

Conduct interviews with:
- 5–8 founders / potential buyers;
- 2–3 experienced equity lawyers or startup counsel;
- 1–2 incubator / accelerator or ecosystem operators.

Questions:
- Describe the last real equity-design task.
- What triggered it?
- What information was difficult to collect?
- What alternatives were used?
- Where was professional time spent?
- What were the most expensive or risky mistakes?
- Who selected and paid the provider?
- What would make an AI-native product trustworthy enough to use?
- Which outputs require a lawyer?
- What would the customer pay for, and on what basis?

Interview counts are planning targets, not evidence of demand.

## 4. Pilot journey

1. Consent, scope and data-use explanation.
2. Workspace and team setup.
3. Structured questionnaire and missing-information loop.
4. Generate scenarios with explicit assumptions.
5. Compare calculations and implications.
6. User comprehension / usefulness check.
7. Professional review for matters requiring it.
8. Deliver report and record feedback.
9. Ask for payment / paid continuation.
10. Record usage, cost, review effort and unresolved risks.

## 5. Metrics

| Dimension | Measure |
|---|---|
| Activation | proportion of invited teams completing intake |
| Task completion | proportion completing the agreed scenario workflow |
| Usability | time to complete, missing-field loops, abandonment |
| Professional quality | evaluator score, critical errors, assumption traceability |
| Safety | appropriate block / escalation rate, data permission incidents |
| Value | user-rated decision clarity; comparison with prior process |
| Commercial | paid pilot, cash collected, repeat task or referral |
| Economics | model/tool cost, review time, support time, gross margin estimate |

Capture a baseline from the customer's prior process where feasible.

## 6. Commercial signal

Minimum target: seek at least one genuinely paid pilot. Stronger target: two paid pilots. No fixed price should be asserted until buyer interviews, scope and alternatives are understood.

Free use may help product learning but is not paid-market validation. An expression of interest, letter of intent or demo feedback is not equivalent to cash received.

## 7. Rights and privacy

- Use coded customer IDs in the repository.
- Keep identifiable and confidential customer data outside GitHub.
- Define permitted data use in the customer agreement.
- Use the smallest necessary data set.
- Keep customer data isolated by tenant / project.
- Do not train models or reuse data across customers unless separately authorized.
- Build test cases from synthetic or explicitly approved materials.
- Define retention and deletion before pilot start.

## 8. Exit criteria

Proceed to broader productization only if:
- target users can complete the task;
- professional evaluators accept the scoped output and escalation behavior;
- deterministic calculations pass;
- there are no unresolved critical rights / data issues;
- at least one paid pilot is secured or there is a clearly evidenced reason for a short, capped iteration;
- per-task costs and human review are measured;
- next investment gate is supported by evidence.

Otherwise revise the task, narrow scope, hold or stop.
